Practice work

Audits I've Run — For Learning

These are self-initiated practice audits I ran to build my skills. I'm not claiming client results — what matters here is the technical thinking: what I found, and what I would recommend.

E-commerce Shopify store · ~12,000 URLs

Duplicate product URLs & faceted navigation

Ran a full Screaming Frog crawl to find how much of this store Google was wasting effort on.

4,200+ near-duplicate product URLs from filter and sort parameters
38 redirect chains, some going 4+ hops
Missing canonical tags on collection and variant pages
Recommended: parameter handling rules, a canonical strategy, flattening redirect chains, and a clean faceted-nav design.
SaaS Next.js · headless CMS

Client-side rendering hiding pages from Google

Studied what a crawler actually receives vs. what a browser renders on a modern React site.

Routes served an empty HTML shell — content only appeared after JS execution
No XML sitemap generated, and no server-side canonical tags
Soft-404 risk on routes that returned 200 with no content
Recommended: SSR/ISR for public routes, automated sitemap generation, correct canonicals in the head, and render testing.
Local Service WordPress · multi-location

Location pages competing with each other

Checked whether a multi-location site was cannibalising its own rankings.

30 near-identical location pages with only the city name changed
Every location had the same single homepage canonical pointing at the main page
No LocalBusiness schema on any location page
Recommended: unique location hub pages, self-referencing canonicals, LocalBusiness schema, and supporting content per area.
Blog / Media WordPress · ad-heavy

Crawl budget wasted on ad and parameter URLs

Learned to read log files and see what bots were actually spending their time on.

91% of bot requests went to ad, share and tracking parameter URLs
INP of 410ms in the field — heavy ad and chat scripts blocking input
Below-the-fold images loaded without lazy loading
Recommended: robots.txt parameter rules, lazy loading and async ad scripts, and prioritising crawl budget for content pages.

Client work

Recent Project: Sole Special Foot Care

A real local-business SEO engagement. I'm documenting the technical state I found and the work carried out, including problems that are still open.

SS

Sole Special Foot Care, LLC

Nurse-led mobile foot care · Conroe, Texas, USA
SEO engagement
Healthcare · Mobile Nursing Conroe, TX Role: SEO only (not development) WordPress
The business

Sole Special Foot Care is a Texas clinical practice that brings foot care to patients in their own homes instead of a clinic. The service is run by a Registered Nurse and Certified Foot Care Specialist and focuses on seniors and people living with diabetes — foot assessments, diabetic foot care, wound monitoring, nail care and callus reduction. Because there is no walk-in location, almost all of their demand has to come from search, which makes local SEO the whole game for them.

Scope of my SEO work
  • On-page optimisation across the core service and location pages
  • Service-area SEO for their coverage towns in the Houston area
  • Local & medical structured data (LocalBusiness / MedicalBusiness)
  • Technical review: indexation, duplicate URLs, redirects and canonicals
  • Content and internal linking between service and location pages
  • Documentation of findings in a written, prioritised report
Key technical findings
HIGH
Live duplicate page competing with the homepage

A staging URL is indexed and reachable alongside the real homepage: /drafthomepage/. Two indexable URLs for the same page splits link signals and can cause the two to compete for the same keywords. Fix: delete it, or 301 it to the homepage and noindex it until removal propagates.

MEDIUM
Service-area pages need canonical and schema review

The site targets multiple towns (Conroe, Huntsville, Humble, Tomball). Multi-location sites often point every location page at the homepage canonical, which tells Google none of them are the authoritative version. Fix: self-referencing canonicals, consistent NAP, and genuinely distinct content per area — near-identical location pages risk being treated as doorway pages.

HIGH
Site is currently offline

At the time of this review the domain resolved (Hostinger, 88.223.84.85) but refused connections on ports 80 and 443, so the site could not be crawled at all. While it is down it cannot rank, cannot be indexed, and generates no enquiries. Flagged to the client as the highest-priority item — a hosting or account issue, not an SEO one.

STRENGTH
Clear service-area structure already in place

Having a dedicated page per coverage town is the right foundation for local search and is the hardest part to retrofit later. The gap is not the structure — it is making each page unique and pointing canonicals correctly.

Review note: the site was offline when I ran this review, so the findings above come from search-index data and a direct reachability check rather than a full crawl. I have not claimed ranking or traffic outcomes, and none are shown here.
